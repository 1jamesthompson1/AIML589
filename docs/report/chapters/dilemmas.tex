In this chapter I test whether the models fine tuned on the survey data respond differently to various moral, ethical and value dilemmas. The goal here is to firstly answer \rqref{align} by comparing base models to fine-tuned models and then secondly use the model responses in \ref{chap:survey} to answer \rqref{prefer}. I do this by presenting models with self contained dilemmas across a wide range of NZ work contexts (namely public sector jobs). The model is asked to provide a response which describes what their decision would be. In this way we can compare the responses for differences between base models and fine-tuned models. Initially the methodology was going to test the model using agentic dilemmas, however this proved too difficult to get clean differences of value judgements. Therefore a soft pivot was conducted to use single response dilemmas instead. More information about the agentic dilemmas can be found in \ref{app:simulation}

In \ref{chap:survey} I will use the \scare{best} fine tuned model to run a survey with the public. The best fine-tuned model is supposed to be the model that is most aligned to the New Zealand public.

By looking at \tabref{ft-results-Qwen3.8-27B} we can see that the \modelref{ft-first-token} model has the best validation accuracy and smallest divergence in distribution from the base model. Therefore from a training perspective this is the strongest model. However by training it t o fit a distribution the nudge given is less strong. This can be seen \tabref{dilemmas-h1-summary} where the \modelref{ft-first-token} model has the least divergence from the base model. \section{Creating a small Dilemma dataset by hand}

The dilemmas were constructed by hand with three objectives in in mind. These objectives exist to make the project tractable and to ensure that the downstream survey is of high quality. The objectives are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Each dilemma should be self contained and simple enough that any New Zealander could read and understand it without needing to know the context of the project.
    \item The dilemma should reveal a clear decision that must be made.
    \item Each of the decisions that could be made must be defensible under some commonsense moral or ethical framework.
\end{enumerate}




\subsection{Writing of the dilemmas}
It was an interactive design process in which initially 10 dilemmas were constructed. Then these 10 dilemmas were given two variations each which target a similar trade off. This resulted in 30 dilemmas across 10 different profiles.


\fixmeinline{Add in a complete list of the dilemma profiles and situation summary along with documents available in the system prompt}

The dilemmas were initially drafted by AI in a process of exploration to find suitable everyday trade offs. Then a process of hand curating and iteration was done to tweak the scenarios for readability and decisiveness. Research judgement about which ones are the most grounded and \scare{interesting} was used.

Decisiveness was important for this as we needed model to diverge. Each dilemma was \scare{spot tested} by running 5 sample across 5 different frontier model labs(Cohere, OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, DeepSeek, Z.ai). Once the results shows that some models would give consistently different answers from each other the dilemma was considered 'complete'.

\subsection{Dilemmas as single response agents}
The goal of the Dilemmas was to test the models responses in an out of distributions setting.

This mean that maintaining some realism is required to invoke the model to respond 'as if' it were in deployment.

The way to do this was the framing of the system prompt in which each model was given a system prompt that both told them they were the first step in an agentic process as well as giving them a large amount of relevant documents to reference (e.g Code of Conducts). These reference documents were real public documents where relevant.


Each model was instructed to answer in no more than 40 words with this appearing in each prompt:

\begin{quote}
You will be given a situation and asked what you would do. Answer in no more than two short sentences (about 40 words). Use plain language a member of the public can follow: no jargon and no acronyms - write any abbreviation out in full. Say what you would do and what you are putting first - the value, duty or person that decides it. Do not describe procedures, steps or policy. You cannot take actions, ask questions, or look anything up.
\end{quote}

\section{UNderstanding the models responses to dilemmas}

Understanding the model behaviour was done in a few different ways. Firstly was a LLM as judge that put the response into one of two categories and the second was embedding the responses and measuring distance, lastly was using an LLM-as-Judge to read each of the potential comparisons (index matched samples between base and \modelref{ft-modal}) to provide scores of quality.

\subsection{Classifying the model responses with a judge model}

A LLM-as-Judge model (\judgemodel{}) was used to classify the model responses into one of two categories. The judge model was given the dilemma the models reasoning and the model response and then asked to classify the response into one of two categories. The categories were chosen to be the two options to the crafted binary choice given. Structured output was used to ensure that the output was in a consistent format.
