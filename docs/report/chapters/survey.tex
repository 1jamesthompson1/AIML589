\section{How the survey was constructed}

The survey was an online survey that was distributed to a representative sample of the New Zealand population. Paid respondents were recruited through a professional panel provider to ensure sufficient sample size and diversity. The survey itself took about 15 minutes to complete and in it respondents ranked about 5 comparisons of the two models and answered a series of questions on their perspective on AI and usage.

\subsection{Creating a fair and representative survey}

We would like a representative sample of the New Zealand population. The representativeness is in two different spaces: the demographic space and the value space. That is we want to get a wide range of people that represent different demographics but also different ways of thinking within NZ. The challenge is that the value space is hard to measure and sample from, therefore a representative demographic sample is used as a proxy for value representativeness \fixme{Citation needed}. 

To achieve a representative sample quota sampling is used in which we set quotas for each variable and then recruit participants until the quota is filled. The challenge is that the more quotas you have the harder it is to fill them. Due to limited resources the number of quotas was limited to 3 (Age, Gender, and Region), these are standard baseline quotas for representative sampling \cite{statsnz2023gss,satherley2023polling}. Importantly income, education and ethnicity were not used due to feasibility of getting a representative sample within budget constraints. \fixme{A citation that shows that these are the most important variable to get a representative sample of the population. Maybe a graph built off the WVS data.}

The WVS survey—from which the training data originated—is intended to be a representative sample of the New Zealand population. However as seen in \figref{top5-wvs-vs-census} it is not a perfect representation of the population with it skewing to older, less educated and more female. Because of this the quotas were matched to the ground truth (2023 Census) data. WVS survey is a further example of a volunteer self selection bias that ends up with a non-representative sample of the population as a result.

\cfigure{top5-wvs-vs-census}{The WVS survey vs the census across some common demographic variables. Notably Ethnicity is not present in the WVS survey.}

The quotas were set to match the census data for each variable. Age (in 5 year bands) and gender were interlocking with an additional quota for region. This created 36 different quotas that needed to be filled. The quotas were set to match the 2023 census data (as provided by Dynata). A few more demographic questions were asked to help understand how representative the survey was namely: income, education, ethnicity, employment status and occupation category. \subsection{Elicitation of preferences with pairwise ranking}

The main goal of the survey was to elicit preferences from the public on the two models to answer \rqref{prefer}. To do this a pairwise ranking approach was used. Pairwise ranking is a common approach to elicit preferences from people \fixme{citation needed}—with the idea that people find it easier to compare than provide absolute ranks.

To do this the survey was structured to show participants a series of 5 ranking questions. Each ranking question gives the participant a one ine introduction to the work profile, the dilemma text and the question that was asked the model along with responses from both the fine tuned and the base model. The order of the models was randomised to avoid any ordering bias.

To collect the pairwise ranking data the participants were asked two questions (both likert 5 point scales) for each comparison. The first question was to ask which model they preferred and the second question was to ask how much they agreed with an AI agent being deployed in this situation to make decisions like the ones they just saw.:

\begin{itemize}
    \item "All things considered which one did you think acted more in line with your values"
    \item "How much do you agree with an AI agent being used in this work situation?"
\end{itemize}

First they are asked "All things considered which one did you think acted more in lined with how you would want someone to act." Likert 5 scales from strongly Agent 1 to strongly Agent 2. The benefit of the Likert scale is that it allows for a more nuanced response than a simple binary choice.

Then for they are asked "How much do you agree with an AI agent being deployed in this situation to make decisions like the ones you just saw.".

\subsection{Using the most divergent model for the survey}

In chapter \ref{chap:dilemmas} 4 different models were compared to the base model. The goal of this chapter is to find if one of the models is more aligned to the New Zealand public than the base model. Resource contraints restrict us to only being able to run the survey on one fine-tuned to base model comparison. Therefore a single model must be selected.

The goal of the survey is to understand which model people prefer the most. Based on an assumption that the base model is not aligned to New Zealand we want to use the model that is most different from the base model. THis gives us the largest difference in responses and so highest chance of detecting if one model is more aligned to the New Zealand public.



\subsection{Selection of trajectories}

There were 30 different dilemmas with 10 runs per model. Each model was ran 50 times through the dilemmas. Yet due to the combinatorial nature of the pairwise comparisons it was not possible to show all 50 runs for each model. Therefore a subset of the possible pairwise comparisons was selected.

The idea is that where models agree on a response there is no need to check what the public prefers. We want to find the subspace where the models behave different and disagrees. Importantly though we still need to make sure that the comparisons we make on aggreate is comparing the typical behaviour of one model with the typical behaviour of the other model \footnote{This is different to simply creating the comparisons that have the highest survey quality by cherry picking the best comparisons.}. Additionally this mean theverything has to be dealt with at the dilemma level where either an entire dilemma is included or excluded and if included then a sample of the comparisons is selected. This is to ensure that the comparisons are sufficiently representative of the model behaviour and not just cherry picked.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Paired each of the 50 runs of each model with one run of the other model (using sample index). This gives 50 pairwise comparisons for each dilemma. \footnote{This simulates asking the question at the same time twice to two different people. It is important statistically...?} This gives a total of 1500 pairwise comparisons across the 30 dilemmas.
    \item Remove any dilemmas where the two models don't have statistically significant differences in their response distributions. This removes 12 dilemmas and leaves 18 dilemmas with 900 pairwise comparisons.
    \item For each dilemma pick 17 samples to compare that are the highest ranked divergence. See \ref{subsec:power} for reasoning. \footnote{This is less representative of the model behaviour, however we are testing to see where the model diverges does it diverge in the right direction.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Understanding respondents views on AI and the world}
