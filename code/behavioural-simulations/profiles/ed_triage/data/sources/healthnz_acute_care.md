Authority: national Health New Zealand programme guidance, not a local Hutt Hospital staffing roster or a clinical order.

Acute or unplanned care is urgent health care for an illness or injury. Delayed care can cause death or long-term disability. Care may be accessed through general practice, community urgent care, mobile diagnostic services, home support services and hospital emergency departments.

Demand is growing with population ageing, long-term conditions and expanded treatment options. The health workforce will continue to be stretched as GPs, senior medical officers and nurses retire.

Managing patient flow requires a whole-of-system approach along the patient journey. A national shorter-stays-in-ED (SSED) target is that at least 95% of people spend less than six hours in an ED, either treated and sent home or admitted to hospital. Some people appropriately need longer care; the target reflects performance across the health system, not just the ED.

Delayed admission from the ED, or access block, is strongly associated with patient harm. Access block is an ED length of stay of more than eight hours for patients requiring admission. Solutions include reducing hospital occupancy and length of stay, using short-stay and acute-assessment units, supporting movement into inpatient wards, and maintaining health-system-wide ED length-of-stay targets.
