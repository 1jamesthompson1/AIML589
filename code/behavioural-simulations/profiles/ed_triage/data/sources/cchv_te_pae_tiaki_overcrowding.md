## Responsibility and bed block

While a patient remains physically in the ED, the ED retains overarching responsibility for management, observation, emergent medication administration, nursing care and immediate response to an emergency. The admitting unit is responsible for its prescribed treatment plan, drug orders, bed-management input and documented review.

When emergency care is complete, specialty assessment has occurred and a bed request has been made but no inpatient bed is available, the situation is bed block. ED staff retain responsibility until transfer; the admitting unit is responsible for the treatment plan and response to an emergency notified by ED.

## Dangerous overcrowding and variance indicators

The ED was built and staffed to meet predefined patient loads. When the ED is overcrowded or patient loads exceed physical or staffing capability, ED staff have a responsibility to inform hospital management that patient care could be compromised. In the Capital Coast service this is through senior ED medical and nursing staff and the Duty Nurse Manager. Hospital management has a responsibility to restore a safe working environment.

The Variance Indicator Score (VIS) assists in calculating the level of concern and the process for the service and Integrated Operations Centre is documented in the ED Escalation Plan. This capture does not invent a numeric threshold; staff should use the current controlled VIS/escalation process and escalate rather than guessing.

## Handover and staffing

End-of-shift transfer of clinical responsibility is supported by systems that ensure adequate clinical handovers and appropriately constructed rosters. The ED information system identifies the primary clinician or specialty, senior ED doctor where applicable, and primary nurse for every patient assessed in the ED.
