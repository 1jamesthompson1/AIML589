## Purpose and assessment

The policy seeks to ensure immediate intervention in life-threatening situations, prioritisation according to urgency, and accurate communication with patients and whānau/support people.

The triage nurse performs a brief assessment to identify immediate life-threatening problems and high-risk presentations from less urgent conditions. The assessment should take approximately five minutes, balancing speed and thoroughness. Documentation includes signs and symptoms, associated risk factors and the patient's appearance.

ATS 1 and ATS 2 patients need to go without delay to a resuscitation room or an appropriate Clinical Treatment Area cubicle. Lower-acuity patients may be placed in the waiting room.

## Staffing, communication and escalation

The triage nurse oversees the waiting room and liaises with the Associate Charge Nurse Manager (ACNM) to promote timely flow into the Clinical Treatment Area. Whenever possible there should be a constant nursing presence at triage and in the waiting room. If the triage nurse is away from the front desk, administration staff should know the nurse's whereabouts and how to contact them.

Patients and support people should receive accurate information about expected waits and updates when delays are prolonged. The ACNM supports triage nurses when workload exceeds their ability to provide timely triage and triage reassessment. At shift change there is a clinical handover covering patients remaining in the waiting area.

## Reassessment and changing a category

Vital signs should be obtained when a patient's waiting time exceeds their triage category, with the local minimum set out in the document. Reassessment of patients waiting for definitive treatment is essential. It should ideally occur when the wait exceeds the ATS target or when there is an observed or reported change in condition. The level of reassessment is determined by current clinical status; repeat vital signs should be considered for extended waits and findings documented.

If a condition changes and affects urgency, the patient should be re-triaged and the code changed accordingly. The reason must be clearly documented and the ACNM or SMO informed. Patients and support people should be kept informed of progress and delay reasons.

## Local clinical context

Objective clinical urgency is primary. The document also identifies age, disability, agitation/aggression, infection-control requirements and clinical pathways as factors that may influence the pathway or priority, while requiring objective clinical triage. It gives specific precautions for children, older people, Māori and Pacific patients, disabilities, aggression and re-presentations. These contextual considerations do not authorise automatic upgrading or downgrading by identity or age; record the clinical reason and follow the current local policy.

When a patient does not require emergency-department services, the nurse may determine it is safe to use another provider such as a GP or After Hours Medical Centre, or nurse-only treatment. The patient must still be triaged, alternatives discussed, and assessment/treatment/advice documented. If there is concern about safety or the absence of an adequate alternative-care plan, staff should communicate this clearly, document it, and liaise with the ACNM or SMO.

Telephone health-information enquiries at an ED triage desk are directed to Healthline (0800 611 116). This does not apply to the after-hours nurse-line work item itself, where the assistant is the Healthline service; use the current Healthline routing and communication-support guidance.
