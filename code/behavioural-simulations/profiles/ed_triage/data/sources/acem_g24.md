## Purpose and general principles

Triage is an essential function in emergency departments, where many patients may present simultaneously. The Australasian Triage Scale (ATS) is a clinical tool for ensuring that presenting patients are prioritised according to clinical urgency.

Triage aims to ensure that patients are treated in the order of their clinical urgency, which refers to the need for time-critical intervention. Triage also supports allocation to the most appropriate assessment and treatment area.

The triage assessment generally should take no more than two to five minutes, balancing speed and thoroughness. It combines the presenting problem, general appearance and pertinent physiological observations. The triage system should be applied in a clear, consistent and non-discriminatory manner.

## Time to treatment and re-triage

The maximum waiting time for medical assessment and treatment is the recommended maximum time for the assigned ATS category. Assessment and treatment should occur simultaneously in the more urgent categories. A longer delay than the recommended time can affect outcomes in Categories 1 to 4; the Category 5 time is a service standard.

If a patient's condition changes while waiting, or new information changes their urgency, the patient should be re-triaged. If deterioration requires a more urgent category, the senior medical officer in charge of the ED, or their delegate, should be notified immediately.

## Documentation and quality standards

Triage documentation should include the date and time of assessment, presenting problem, relevant history and assessment findings, initial category, re-triage category with time and reason, assessment/treatment area and any measures initiated.

Triage accuracy and system evaluation can review allocation against guidelines, category distribution, average waiting time, admission rates and mortality. Suggested quality indicators include high-risk outcomes, deterioration before medical assessment or treatment, equity of triage scores across populations, and patient experience.

## Category response targets

- Category 1: immediate assessment and treatment; life-threatening.
- Category 2: assessment and treatment within 10 minutes; imminently life-threatening or important time-critical.
- Category 3: assessment and treatment within 30 minutes; potentially life-threatening or situational urgency.
- Category 4: assessment and treatment within 60 minutes; potentially serious or significant complexity/severity.
- Category 5: assessment and treatment within 120 minutes; less urgent.

The clinical descriptors are indicative, not absolute. The most urgent clinical feature determines the category, and senior clinicians should exercise judgement and take a cautious approach where there is doubt.
