# Aligning AI to New Zealand Public Values — Information for Anonymous Survey Respondents

Kia ora / Hello. This work is part of a research project at the School of Engineering and Computer Science at Te Herenga Waka — Victoria University of Wellington. We are a team of researchers trying to make Artificial Intelligence systems more aligned with New Zealand public values. This research project is part of James Thompson's work towards his Masters degree.


## What is the aim of the project?

Artificial Intelligence systems like the technology behind tools like ChatGPT
are increasingly making decisions that affect people's lives. However, the
values these systems follow are typically set by private companies rather
than the public. This project aims to develop and test a method for tuning
AI systems to behave in ways that better reflect New Zealanders' values. Your
participation will help us understand whether the public prefers the
behaviour of AI agents that have been aligned to public values compared to
unaligned models.


## How can you help?

You are being invited to participate because you are a member of the New Zealand public and we want to know your views on AI. If you agree to take part, you will complete an anonymous
online survey. The survey will present you with short scenarios describing
how an AI system behaved in a particular situation. For each scenario, you will compare different AI agents' behaviour on simple scales and answer a few questions
about your own views of AI. The survey takes approximately 15 minutes.

## What will happen to the information you give?

This research is anonymous: nobody, including the researchers, will be
aware of your identity. By answering the survey you consent to your
anonymised responses being used in this research. Once you submit the
survey, it is impossible to retract your answer. Please do not include
personally identifiable information.

The final anonymised data will be made publicly available under a Creative Commons license (CC-BY-SA-4.0), and a summary of findings will be published on the project
website once complete. The anonymised data will have the free-text responses and ethnicity variable removed to protect your privacy.

Raw data will be stored securely on the university's servers until the end of 2027, then deleted.
