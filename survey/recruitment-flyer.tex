\noindent\textcolor{teal}{\textbf{What would you do?}}
\small

\noindent You'll read short scenarios where an AI makes a real-world decision like prioritising patients in an emergency room or screening job
applicants. You'll rate how much you agree with the AI's actions in a handful of scenarios, along with some simple questions about yourself.

\vspace{0.4em}

\noindent\textcolor{teal}{\textbf{Why participate?}}
\small

\noindent Your voice will directly shape research on how AI should behave in
Aotearoa. Right now most AI models are developed by for-profit companies
overseas. These AI models are then used in the real world to take actions and make decision that could end up effecting you! We want to show how public institutions can create AI that reflects the values of the communities they
serve. If you love AI, hate AI or are indifferent you should be apart of how it is developed and influences your life.

\vspace{0.4em}

\noindent You can optionally enter a separate prize draw for one of two \$50
Prezzy Cards. To enter, you will provide an email address in a separate form.
This email address will not be linked to your survey responses.

\noindent\textcolor{teal}{\textbf{How your responses will be used}}
\small

\noindent Your answers are completely anonymous. We'll analyse them to see
which AI model outputs the public prefers, then release the anonymised data
into the public domain so the global research community can benefit too. The
end goal is a practical, repeatable way for public institutions to align AI
with community values.

\vspace{0.6em}

\noindent\textcolor{teal}{\textbf{How the project works}}
\small

\noindent We started with New Zealand data from the World Values Survey a global research project that asks people about their beliefs and values. We
used this data to fine-tune an open-weight Large Language Model (the AI behind ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot etc.), then ran behavioural
simulations in an agentic framework, asking the model to make decisions in
real-world scenarios. Now, we're asking the New Zealand public to compare
the aligned and unaligned model outputs and tell us which feels more right. The hope being that users will prefer the aligned model outputs, and thus show a practical way to create AI that is better aligned with the communities it serves.
